# WeWork — Due Diligence Brief (2023 Bankruptcy Analysis)

## Company Profile

**WeWork Global LLC**
- **Founded:** 2010 | **HQ:** New York, NY
- **Sector:** Flexible workspace / PropTech
- **Stage:** Public (NYSE: WE) — filed Chapter 11 November 2023
- **Peak Valuation:** $47B (2019) → Bankruptcy (2023)

## Executive Summary

WeWork pioneered the flexible workspace industry, offering beautifully designed shared office spaces to startups, freelancers, and enterprise clients. Despite creating a category-defining brand and generating $3.2B in annual revenue, the company filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in November 2023 due to unsustainable lease obligations, rapid overexpansion, and governance failures.

## Root Cause Analysis

### 1. Structural Lease Obligations
- **Total lease commitments:** $27B+ (long-term, non-cancellable)
- **Average lease term:** 10-15 years
- **Revenue per lease dollar:** $0.40 (meaning for every $1 of lease, WeWork generates $0.40 of revenue)
- **The math doesn't work** — lease costs exceed revenue at scale

### 2. Rapid Overexpansion
- Opened 400+ locations in 24 months (2018-2019)
- Many locations were unprofitable from day one
- signed leases in secondary markets with weak demand
- Competing with own locations for tenants

### 3. Governance Failures
- CEO Adam Neumann's related-party transactions ($7M consulting fee from WeWork)
- Board composition and oversight concerns
- Lack of independent board members
- Repeated restatements of financial metrics

### 4. Market Timing
- IPO attempted at $47B valuation in 2019 — failed
- Pandemic reduced office demand permanently (hybrid work)
- Interest rate hikes increased cost of capital
- Enterprise clients downsized office footprints

## Key Lessons for Due Diligence

1. **Lease vs. revenue ratio** — WeWork's lease obligations were 8x revenue
2. **Unit economics matter** — Each location was unprofitable at scale
3. **Governance risk** — Related-party transactions and weak board oversight
4. **Market timing** — Pandemic accelerated structural decline in office demand
5. **Valuation discipline** — $47B peak vs. $0 at bankruptcy

## Recommendation

**Not Investable — Distressed / Bankruptcy**

WeWork represents a cautionary tale in due diligence:
- Strong brand and market position couldn't overcome structural economics
- Lease obligations created insurmountable fixed costs
- Governance failures prevented timely course correction
- Market timing (pandemic, rates) accelerated inevitable decline

**For due diligence teams:** The WeWork case demonstrates why lease analysis, unit economics, and governance review are critical in any commercial real estate or workspace investment.
