# Hivedesk — Due Diligence Brief (2023 Bankruptcy Analysis)

> **Fictional company, written for the Sift demo.** The company, its people and every figure are invented. It is not an analysis of any real company's filings.

## Company Profile

**Hivedesk Holdings Inc.**
- **Founded:** 2012 | **HQ:** New York, NY
- **Sector:** Flexible workspace / PropTech
- **Stage:** Listed via SPAC (2021) — filed Chapter 11 in October 2023
- **Peak Valuation:** $18B private valuation (2019) → Bankruptcy (2023)

## Executive Summary

Hivedesk built a well-known brand in flexible workspace, leasing whole floors on long terms and re-letting desks and private offices on short ones to startups, freelancers and enterprise teams. Despite $1.4B in annual revenue, it filed for Chapter 11 in October 2023: long-term lease obligations it could not cut, rapid overexpansion, and governance failures.

## Root Cause Analysis

### 1. Structural Lease Obligations
- **Total lease commitments:** $11B (long-term, non-cancellable), about 8x annual revenue
- **Average lease term:** 12 years, against member contracts that average 9 months
- **Annual rent:** $1.6B in FY2023, more than the $1.25B of revenue
- **The math doesn't work** — fixed rent exceeds revenue even at target occupancy

### 2. Rapid Overexpansion
- Opened 160 locations in 24 months (2018–2019)
- Many locations were unprofitable from opening
- Signed leases in secondary markets with weak demand
- New sites competed with the company's own nearby locations

### 3. Governance Failures
- Founder-CEO leased three buildings he part-owned back to the company ($12M a year in rent)
- Board lacked a majority of independent directors until 2020
- A non-standard "community-adjusted" profit metric was dropped after investor criticism
- Two restatements of occupancy figures

### 4. Market Timing
- IPO attempt in 2019 at an $18B target was withdrawn
- The pandemic and hybrid work reduced demand for office space
- Interest-rate rises increased the cost of refinancing
- Enterprise clients cut their office footprints

## Competitive Landscape

| Competitor model | How it works | Advantage over Hivedesk |
|------------------|--------------|-------------------------|
| Franchise operators | Local partners carry the leases | Lower capital intensity |
| Management contracts | Landlord owns the risk, operator takes a fee | No lease liability |
| Landlord-run flex floors | Owners fit out their own space | No rent margin to cover |
| Enterprise-only providers | Long contracts with large tenants | Predictable occupancy |

## Key Lessons for Due Diligence

1. **Lease vs. revenue ratio** — lease obligations were 8x revenue
2. **Duration mismatch** — 12-year leases funded by 9-month member contracts
3. **Governance risk** — related-party leases and a weak board
4. **Market timing** — hybrid work accelerated a structural decline
5. **Valuation discipline** — $18B private peak vs. negative equity at filing

## Recommendation

**Not Investable — Distressed / Bankruptcy**

- A strong brand could not overcome structural unit economics
- Lease obligations created fixed costs the business could not cut
- Governance failures delayed a course correction
- Hybrid work and higher rates accelerated the decline

**For due diligence teams:** lease analysis, unit economics and governance review are the first three checks for any workspace or commercial real estate investment.
